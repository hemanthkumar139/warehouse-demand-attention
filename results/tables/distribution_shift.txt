Distribution Shift Experiment
==============================

Original distribution:
Noise standard deviation = 5
Spike probability = 0.01
Spike scale = 50

Shifted distribution:
Noise standard deviation = 15
Spike probability = 0.03
Spike scale = 100

Last-value baseline:

Original:
MAE  = 7.6552
RMSE = 11.0973

Shifted:
MAE  = 21.4833
RMSE = 31.0841

Change:
MAE increase  = 13.8281
RMSE increase = 19.9868

Interpretation:
The shifted distribution contains substantially more noise and larger,
more frequent demand spikes. The baseline error increased substantially
under this distribution shift.

The increase in RMSE is particularly notable because RMSE penalizes
large errors more strongly than MAE. Larger spikes therefore have a
greater effect on RMSE.

Conclusion:
The experiment demonstrates that even a simple strong baseline can
degrade substantially when the statistical properties of demand change.
